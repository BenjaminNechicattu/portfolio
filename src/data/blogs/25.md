People say that confession brings closure.

Maybe it does.

But nobody talks about what comes after.

Nobody tells you how difficult it is to see the same person every day and convince yourself that you're okay.

Every time I try to spend time with her, something seems to get in the way. A meeting. A call. Someone else. A reason to leave. Maybe they're genuine reasons. Maybe I'm simply noticing them more now.

I know she doesn't owe me her time.
I know she has every right to choose the people she wants around her.

So why do I keep feeling... unseen?

Maybe I'm overthinking.
Or maybe rejection makes you search for meaning in places where there isn't any.
I wait for the conversations that never happen.
I wait for the moments I imagine we'd have.
I wait for a version of us that only exists in my head.

Someone once told me, "Women don't need someone to complete them."
They're right. But I wasn't trying to complete her.

I only wished I could be someone who made her day a little brighter.
I tried.

At least, I think I did.
Sometimes I wonder if there was anything more I could have done.

Then something small happens.

Someone gives her a gift.
She smiles.

And I catch myself feeling jealous—not because of the gift, but because someone else got to create that smile.

It isn't my proudest feeling.
But it's an honest one.

She tells stories.

About her family.

Her work.

Her plans.

Her dreams.

I often hear them through conversations with others, not with me.
Maybe that's simply where I belong in her life.
Or maybe my heart keeps expecting a place that was never mine.

Sometimes I even laugh at myself.
"Maybe I'm just a possessive, toxic guy." 😂

But deep down, I know it isn't about possession.
It's about longing.
Longing for moments that never happened.

Moments my heart quietly writes over reality.

That should have been me making her laugh.

That should have been me listening to her stories at the end of the day.

That should have been me driving nowhere, just because she wanted company.

That should have been me buying her roses.

That should have been me holding her hand.

That should have been me she leaned on when life felt heavy.

That should have been me she hugged when life felt beautiful.

That should have been me she sent the last "Good night" to.

That should have been me.

Or perhaps...

It was never meant to be me.

Maybe that's the hardest part of moving on.

Not letting go of the person But letting go of the life you imagined with them.
